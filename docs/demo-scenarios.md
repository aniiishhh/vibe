# Vibe presentation scenarios

## How to use this script

These are fictional people, memories and conversations for a future presentation. Vibe’s replies below describe the intended response shape. Course offerings, professor evidence, prices, opening hours and competing products should come from the sources retrieved during the eventual demo. Bracketed fields are filled from those results.

Use the same four accounts across the three parties so the audience can follow personal memory carrying across contexts. Each scene can stand alone. A short presentation can show the class search, switch to the trip for cross-party personalization, and finish with one hackathon research exchange.

This document prepares the scenario content. Account setup, memory seeding, messages and research happen in a later simulation session.

## Cast and memory preparation

| Person | Role in the story | Compact personal points to prepare |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Maya | Organizes the group and cares about practical choices | Interested in applied machine learning. Vegetarian. Prefers hands-on projects. |
| Jordan | Keeps the plans realistic | Interested in backend systems. Prefers predictable costs. Comfortable building AWS-backed applications. |
| Priya | Cares about inclusive experiences and sustainable pacing | Interested in human-centered design. Prefers a manageable workload. Enjoys museums and relaxed sightseeing. |
| Leo | Likes building and exploring with friends | Interested in computer vision. Enjoys photography. Prefers contributing frontend work. |

Have each fictional account add and approve its own points through Personal memory. Turn on **Use my approved memory** for each account in each demo party. This gives Vibe permission to retrieve relevant points for the group’s request.

Keep one point per memory entry so a presenter can show its owner, category, approval and correction controls clearly. Select the relevant points in a reply, such as Maya’s vegetarian preference for dining and her machine-learning interest for classes.

The group’s semester, busy times, trip dates, spending limit and hackathon deadline belong in that party’s discussion or shared notes. Keep these temporary constraints attached to the plan they describe.

### Optional memory-learning opening

To show how an automatic memory originates, stage this earlier exchange in a separate casual party with Maya’s learning enabled.

**Jordan**
> Anyone want to grab something after we finish?

**Maya**
> Yes please. I’m vegetarian, so somewhere with a few proper vegetarian mains would be great.

**Priya**
> Works for me. Let’s decide after this.

Later, open Maya’s Personal memory and review the candidate supported by her own message. Approve the vegetarian point before the trip scene. Use this as an alternative to manually adding that point.

Quiet-message extraction becomes eligible after two minutes of inactivity and is discovered on a five-minute schedule. Allow roughly two to seven minutes plus worker latency, or prepare this moment before presenting. A later @Vibe mention also triggers bounded extraction work. Approval is an explicit step before cross-party reuse.

# Scenario 1 — Four people, one class

## Party setup

| Field | Suggested value |
| --- | --- |
| Name | The Tuesday Survivors |
| Goal | Find one Spring 2027 ASU CSE elective that all four of us can take together, balancing interests, meeting times and workload. |
| Vibe mode | Focused |
| Expected automatic theme | Studies, with Pip’s academic accessories |
| Admins | Maya and Jordan |

During creation, show the mode selection and invitation step. Maya creates the invitation link and QR. The other three accounts join. Maya appoints Jordan as another admin.

## Opening conversation

**Maya**
> Can we finally take one elective together? Spring 2027, ASU Tempe, ideally an in-person CSE course at the 400 level.

**Jordan**
> I’m in. I want something useful that we can actually fit around our existing classes.

**Priya**
> Same. A manageable workload matters more to me than finding the most impressive course title.

**Leo**
> I’d love something visual or practical. Give me something we can make.

**Maya**
> I’m unavailable Tuesdays and Thursdays from 9 am to noon.

**Jordan**
> I’m unavailable Mondays, Wednesdays and Fridays before 10 am.

**Priya**
> I’m unavailable Mondays and Wednesdays from 1 pm to 4 pm.

**Leo**
> Fridays after 4 pm are blocked for me. These times are all Arizona time for Spring 2027.

## Beat 1 — Research for the whole group

**Maya**
> @Vibe find up to three real Spring 2027 ASU Tempe CSE electives at the 400 level that we could take together. Use each person’s approved relevant memories and the busy times we just gave you. Show the actual sections and meeting times, explain the trade-offs for all four of us, and flag anything you still need to verify.

### Expected Vibe response shape

- A small set of actual offerings with source links and retrieved section details.
- An explanation connecting the options to Maya’s applied-ML interest, Jordan’s systems interest, Priya’s workload preference and Leo’s computer-vision interest.
- A check against each person’s stated busy intervals for the retrieved meetings. Any unavailable meeting data stays marked unknown.
- A clear distinction between a course that fits everyone’s calendar and one that meets everyone’s preferences.
- Explicit missing prerequisites, enrollment eligibility, seat availability or associated-meeting information where the retrieved evidence is incomplete.

An illustrative sentence could be “This section appears to fit the four busy-time constraints you supplied. Its applied content aligns with Maya and Leo, while its systems component could interest Jordan. Priya’s workload preference needs closer syllabus evidence.” The actual course and supporting claims come from the live results.

## Beat 2 — Narrow the question

Choose one course and instructor from the returned results.

**Jordan**
> @Vibe focus on [returned course]. What can you find in the syllabus about projects, assessments and workload? Prefer Spring 2027 evidence for [returned instructor], and label any older or different-instructor syllabus you use.

### Expected Vibe behavior

Retrieve syllabus evidence for the selected course. Explain the evidence relevant to hands-on work and workload. Show the source term and instructor. If no usable syllabus is retrieved, identify the unanswered questions the group should check.

**Priya**
> @Vibe how is [returned instructor] for [returned course]? Check Rate My Professors and explain recurring comments about teaching, grading and workload. Separate course-specific reviews from the professor’s overall ratings.

### Expected Vibe behavior

Resolve the professor identity, retrieve a bounded review sample and summarize recurring themes with citations. Attribute subjective judgments to reviewers and show sample/coverage limits.

## Beat 3 — Make a decision together

**Leo**
> The project-based option sounds good to me.

**Maya**
> Me too, assuming we meet the prerequisites.

**Priya**, replying directly to Leo’s message
> That sounds fun. I’m in, provided the meeting times stay the same.

**Jordan**
> I can check the prerequisites and any lab requirement.

**Maya**
> @Vibe summarize our current choice and the conditions still open. Propose a task for Jordan to verify prerequisites and associated meetings. Keep the assignment as a proposal until he accepts it.

### What to show

1. Vibe’s sourced decision summary and open conditions.
2. A proposed task for Jordan, followed by Jordan clicking **Accept**.
3. The contextual interpretation of Priya’s reply as agreement with the proposal she replied to.
4. Maya adding a short admin note after the group agrees on its shortlist.

### Presenter line

> “Four people can ask one shared question. Vibe combines the conversation, each person’s approved preferences and live course evidence to help them find common ground.”

# Scenario 2 — The same friends plan a trip

## Party setup

| Field | Suggested value |
| --- | --- |
| Name | Out of Office, Finally |
| Goal | Plan a two-night visit to San Diego from Phoenix, exploring neighborhoods, local attractions and places to eat within a shared budget. |
| Vibe mode | Friendly |
| Expected automatic theme | Travel, with Pip’s travel accessories |
| Admins | Maya and Priya |

Use the same accounts and their approved points. Before the first Vibe request, each person enables approved-memory use in this party. Maya’s vegetarian preference can come from the optional earlier learning scene.

## Opening conversation

**Maya**
> San Diego for November 27–29, 2026? Three days, two nights. We’d leave Phoenix Friday morning and come back Sunday evening.

**Jordan**
> My target is $350 per person for the whole thing, including lodging, transport, meals and activities. Let’s check whether that’s realistic for those dates.

**Priya**
> I’m interested, but I’d like breathing room in the schedule. One big activity a day sounds good.

**Leo**
> I’m in. I can help compare neighborhoods and find places worth stopping for photos.

## Beat 1 — Build a plan with cross-party memory

**Priya**
> @Vibe suggest two ways we could structure this weekend. Use all four people’s approved relevant preferences. Give us an honest budget breakdown, identify the biggest unknowns and check public sources for the attractions you suggest. Start with a compact comparison so we can choose a direction.

### Expected Vibe response shape

- Two coherent approaches, such as a relaxed neighborhood-and-waterfront visit and a museum-focused weekend.
- Maya’s vegetarian preference reflected in the food criteria, without asking her to repeat it.
- Priya’s interest in museums and relaxed sightseeing reflected in pacing.
- Leo’s photography interest reflected in suggested stops.
- Jordan’s preference for predictable costs reflected in itemized estimates, fee questions and budget uncertainty.
- Links to relevant public sources, with live prices and availability qualified according to what was actually retrieved.

Show the memory page briefly so the audience can trace a relevant approved point to its owner and source.

## Beat 2 — Ask a focused web question

**Leo**
> @Vibe compare Balboa Park and the La Jolla waterfront for this group. Check official visitor information for access, hours and any relevant fees. Which fits the relaxed version of our plan better, and where would photography fit naturally?

### Expected Vibe behavior

Use web search and fetch to find relevant public visitor information, cite what it retrieves and make a preference-aware comparison. Keep the response centered on these two choices.

**Maya**
> @Vibe find two vegetarian-friendly dinner options near the area we choose. Use current menu or restaurant information, include links, and flag anything we should confirm before going.

### Expected Vibe behavior

Research restaurants near the selected area, connect the choices to the group’s location and Maya’s preference, and identify uncertain opening hours, prices or booking details from the available evidence.

## Beat 3 — React to a changed constraint

**Jordan**
> Small update for this weekend. I can stretch to $400 if the extra amount means we stay somewhere quieter and avoid extra driving.

**Priya**
> That trade-off works for me. I’d rather spend a little more than rush around all day.

**Maya**
> @Vibe update the relaxed plan using Jordan’s $400 limit for this trip. Show what changes and what still needs a real quote. Propose a task for Leo to compare two lodging options.

**Leo**
> I’ll take that.

Leo then accepts the proposed task in the task controls.

### What to show

1. A new party with a different automatic background and matching Pip accessories.
2. Approved personal preferences reused across contexts.
3. Public-source citations supporting attraction and dining suggestions.
4. The plan adapting to a temporary spending change in this conversation.
5. An actual accepted task, with the proposed assignee taking the action.

### Presenter line

> “The same people move from choosing a class to planning a weekend. Vibe can retrieve their relevant approved preferences again, while this trip keeps its own dates, budget and decisions.”

# Scenario 3 — Research a hackathon idea together

## Party setup

| Field | Suggested value |
| --- | --- |
| Name | What Can We Ship? |
| Goal | Research and choose a small, useful hackathon prototype that fits our team’s skills and the event requirements. |
| Vibe mode | Focused |
| Expected automatic theme | Building, with Pip’s builder accessories |
| Admins | Jordan and Maya |

Jordan adds this short custom party note as an admin.

> Hackathon planning context. Choose a new prototype, preserve attribution for reused tools and AI assistance, and prepare a public repository plus a Devpost write-up. Our presentation should show a working user flow and explain AWS usage. Event rules source https://sunhacks-2026.devpost.com/rules

This supplies the relevant planning context to the party. Use the event requirements recorded in `docs/hackathon.md` when preparing the later simulation.

## Opening conversation

**Jordan**
> Let’s choose something with a clear user problem and a demo we can finish. In this scenario we have four hours left to build the prototype.

**Maya**
> I’d like an idea where the AI helps someone make progress, with a concrete outcome we can show.

**Priya**
> I want the experience to work for someone who isn’t technical. One clear flow would be better than ten unfinished features.

**Leo**
> I can take the frontend. Let’s give ourselves something we can explain through the product itself.

## Beat 1 — Turn individual strengths into a shortlist

**Jordan**
> @Vibe propose three small hackathon ideas using our approved interests and strengths, the four-hour build window and the rules note. For each, give a target user, one core problem, the smallest demoable flow and a reasonable split of work. Explain what AWS and personal memory would contribute where they fit.

### Possible idea directions

These are starting directions for the fictional brainstorming scene. Vibe’s actual suggestions may vary.

| Direction | Target user and problem | Small demoable flow |
| --- | --- | --- |
| BorrowLoop | Students coordinating shared equipment need to know what is available and who is responsible for returning it. | Request an item, compare available options, and confirm a handoff checklist. |
| RepairRelay | A small campus club needs to turn unclear equipment problems into actionable maintenance reports. | Describe a problem, organize the details and propose a checklist for a human maintainer. |
| FirstWeek | New club members struggle to find the next useful onboarding action. | Explain a goal, receive a short onboarding plan and complete one assigned step. |

### Expected Vibe response shape

Use Maya’s practical-ML interest, Jordan’s backend/AWS skills, Priya’s human-centered design interest and Leo’s frontend preference when discussing team fit. Keep proposed assignments visible as suggestions. Identify an implementation scope that could plausibly fit the fictional time window.

## Beat 2 — Research before choosing

**Priya**
> @Vibe research BorrowLoop and FirstWeek. Find relevant existing products or public projects and explain what problem they already solve. Include source links, then suggest one small differentiator we could actually demonstrate for each idea.

### Expected Vibe behavior

Use public web search and fetch to inspect a bounded set of relevant sources. Compare specific observed capabilities and suggest a modest differentiator grounded in the findings. Describe the search coverage and any unanswered questions about competing products.

**Maya**
> @Vibe compare those two ideas for user value, team fit, implementation risk and demo clarity. Recommend one based on what we know, and name the single biggest assumption we should validate first.

### Expected Vibe behavior

Combine the source-backed research, current discussion and approved relevant personal points into a compact decision matrix. Explain the recommendation and the evidence still needed.

## Beat 3 — Convert discussion into accountable work

**Leo**
> FirstWeek feels easier to show as a complete flow. I’d like to build that interface.

**Priya**, replying directly to Leo’s proposal
> That sounds good. I’m in. I can sketch the onboarding flow and write the empty states.

**Jordan**
> I can handle the API and persistence.

**Maya**
> I’ll work on the agent instructions and a small set of examples.

**Jordan**
> @Vibe summarize the decision, the assumption we still need to validate and the smallest version we agreed to build. Propose the four tasks we just volunteered for. Keep each task pending its owner’s acceptance.

Each person accepts their own task. Jordan records the final scope in an admin note.

### What to show

1. Idea suggestions that reflect the four members’ relevant approved interests and skills.
2. A focused web-research follow-up with citations.
3. A recommendation with a stated assumption and visible trade-offs.
4. Priya’s reply connected to the proposal she selected.
5. Four persistent task proposals, followed by four individual acceptances.

### Presenter line

> “Vibe helps the team move from scattered opinions to a researched choice and a plan that people explicitly agree to own.”

# Suggested presentation order

## A short walkthrough

1. Open the class party with all four members visible.
2. Show the initial search request, one sourced reply and a focused professor or syllabus follow-up.
3. Open Maya’s approved vegetarian memory and its source.
4. Switch to the trip party and show how that preference helps shape a researched suggestion.
5. Finish with the hackathon party’s researched decision and accepted tasks.

## An interactive presentation

Give each scene its own turn. Let a teammate send a new constraint or a focused follow-up, then show the answer updating. Keep the next prompt tied to the latest answer, especially when a course, professor, location or idea has been selected from live results.

## Preparation notes for the later simulation

- Prepare the four accounts, owner-approved personal points and per-party sharing settings.
- Use real result values in every bracketed course/instructor field.
- Stage one bounded research question at a time so each response has a clear purpose and enough room for its sources.
- Keep missing source information visible. For example, an unavailable syllabus leaves workload unresolved, and a public lodging page may leave date-specific pricing unanswered.
- Use the app’s actual learning, approval, note and task states when recording the presentation.
- Allow background-learning time before recording its memory-review moment.
- Keep the existing SES sandbox in mind when staging invitations. Link and QR joining are available for the group setup scene.
- Existing parties can receive semantic theme selection through the admin Background control. Theme selection chooses among the seven current designs.
